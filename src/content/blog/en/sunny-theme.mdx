This site's color scheme is called <mark>Sunny</mark>.
It comes from my Fender Vintera II Mustang in <mark>Competition Orange</mark> with red racing stripes.
Those two colors are the primary and the accent color.
The stripe (thin, wide, then thin again, at 45°) shows up everywhere: in the logo, in the hero and on every position on the <a href={route('career', 'en')}>Experience</a> page.

## Six numbers

The whole theme hangs on six numbers: lightness, chroma and hue (<a href="https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/color_value/oklch" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">OKLCH</a>) for the primary and the accent color. Everything else is derived from them – backgrounds, text, borders and the palette above. Even the grays are tinted slightly towards the hue. Because the six numbers are registered as <a href="https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@property" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CSS properties</a> (`@property`), the entire color scheme can change at runtime.

## Light and dark

Every color is defined with `light-dark()` and follows `color-scheme`.
The site follows your system setting; the switch in the header overrides it.
Single sections can be pinned to one mode with the classes <span class="sunny-light">`sunny-light`</span> or <span class="sunny-dark">`sunny-dark`</span>.

## Every position in its own colors

On the <a href={route('career', 'en')}>Experience</a> page, the site takes on each company's colors as you scroll. Brand colors come in every lightness, so they're adjusted: light enough to stay readable on a dark background. When switching between two companies, the colors blend in OKLab instead of rotating the hue. That turns yellow to blue into a short, calm transition instead of a rainbow through red and pink. At the end of the page it returns to Sunny.

## Fonts

Three fonts, three jobs: Fraunces for headings, Inter for body text and JetBrains Mono for details.

**Fraunces** brings the character.
It has two unusual axes: `SOFT` rounds off the edges, `WONK` gives a few letters a slightly quirky shape.
Both are turned all the way up here.
The result is a soft, round serif that recalls '70s advertising posters – the same era as the Mustang.

**Inter** stays in the background.
For longer texts, readability matters more than personality, and Inter is built for exactly that: clear, calm and easy to read even at small sizes.
Next to the playful Fraunces, it keeps things calm.

**JetBrains Mono** covers everything technical: dates, small labels, the prompt in the header (`weblogixx@…`) and, of course, code.
The monospace font recalls the terminal and the editor, and visibly sets metadata apart from the actual text.

On the technical side, a few deliberate choices:

- **Self-hosted.** The fonts come from this site itself, not from Google Fonts or any other service. There's no request to third-party servers, and the <a href={route('privacy', 'en')}>privacy policy</a> stays short.
- **Variable fonts.** One file per font covers all weights, and for Fraunces the `SOFT` and `WONK` axes too.
- **Latin character set only.** It covers English and German, including ä, ö, ü and ß, and keeps the files small.
- **No flicker.** The files are preloaded (`preload`), and `font-display: optional` keeps the text from jumping once the font arrives. If you've been here before, the fonts are cached and show up right away. Only on a very slow first visit does that one page stay in the system font.
